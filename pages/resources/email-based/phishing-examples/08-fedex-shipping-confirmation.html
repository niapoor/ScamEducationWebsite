DELIVERY OF THE SUSPENDED PACKAGE

The all-caps heading “SUSPENDED” does not match the calm subject line above it. When the subject and message do not match, it can be a warning sign of a scam.

Dear Recipient,

A real shipping notice would use your name because the shipping company has it for your delivery. A greeting like “Dear Recipient” is a warning sign that the email may have been sent to many people at once.

We are writing to inform you that a package is currently awaiting delivery to your address. Please use the tracking code below to track and receive your package.

This scam works because many people are expecting a package. Even if you are waiting for a package, check the email before clicking any links. Make sure the details match a package you are expecting.

TRACK PACKAGE

This button leads to a fake tracking page designed to steal your personal information. It is not the real FedEx tracking page. Before clicking a link, check where it leads. On a computer, hover your mouse over the link. On a phone or tablet, press and hold the link to see where it leads.

To ensure hassle-free delivery and avoid future delays, we recommend that you schedule your delivery and subscribe to our calendar notifications.

This line is deliberately styled to look like unimportant fine print, but "subscribing to calendar notifications" from an unknown sender is its own way to keep sending you things — a real carrier doesn't need you to subscribe to anything just to receive a package.

TRACKING CODE: 87430896

Bold red text can make a random number look important and urgent. A real tracking number should work on the shipping company’s website. If you were not expecting the email or it looks suspicious, go to the website yourself and enter the tracking number there. Do not use the link in the email.
