Dear Beloved Friend,A greeting like “Beloved Friend” may sound warm and personal, but it is not. Scammers can use the same greeting when they send the email to many strangers.

It is my pleasure to contact you. I know this letter might come to you as a surprise, but I want you to read through my kind email and hear me well. My name is Mrs. Elizabeth A. Johnson from Germany. I was married to Dr. Abelerd Johnson, who worked with the German Embassy here in South Africa for a period of eight (8) years before he died in an auto crash with our only child.

I am a 59 years old woman suffering from a long time cancer of the breast. Due to my deteriorating health condition, which most doctors have confirmed, I will not survive no matter what.This sad story is meant to make you feel sympathy and trust the sender before they make their request. When you feel sorry for someone, you may be less likely to question what they ask you to do.

Therefore, I have decided to willingly donate the sum of USD $5,600,000.00 (Five Million, Six Hundred Thousand Dollars) to a charity or individual through you, for the good work of God, and to help the motherless, less privileged, and also for the assistance of widows.Being offered a large amount of money by someone you do not know may seem too good to be true. This is a big warning sign. Someone with more than five million dollars to give away would likely work with a lawyer, bank, or other professional to handle the money.

I reach out to you, soliciting that you accept this donation from me on behalf of the less privileged ones in our society, and use the money to set up a charity organization and to take care of the abandoned widows who need help for survival. This appeal is meant for one with a deep sense of responsibility and commitment to serving humanity.

Once I hear from you, I will forward to you all the information you will use to get this fund released from the bank here in South Africa and to be transferred to your bank account.This is how the scam starts. After you reply, the scammer may ask you to pay “release,” “transfer,” or “tax” fees to get the money. The promised money is not real. The fees are how the scammer makes money.

I humbly seek your assistance to receive these funds and use them for the intended purposes. Please get back to me at my email address if you're interested in assisting me on this noble cause.

Love and blessings,

Yours faithfully,
Mrs. Elizabeth A. Johnson
